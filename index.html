Mission

The Institute for Study of Rights and Repression (ISRR) brings together scholars, graduate students, and methodological innovators at the University of New Mexico to study the protection and erosion of rights across democratic and democratizing societies. Our research spans the United States, East Africa, Western Europe, and Northern Ireland, with substantive focus areas in voting rights, redistricting, immigration policy, human rights, gender, religion, and state oppression.

The Institute is also committed to advancing the methods by which these questions are studied. Our affiliated researchers develop open-source statistical software for election analysis and contribute to large-scale AI and natural language processing initiatives for the social sciences.

Research Pillars

Loren Collingwood

Rights in American Democracy

Voting rights, redistricting and representation, immigration policy, race and ethnic politics, and cross-racial coalition building.

Sarah Dreier

Repression & Human Rights

State oppression and democratic backsliding, human rights in East Africa and Northern Ireland, gender, religion, and the politics of internment.

Computational & Statistical Methods

Open-source software for ecological inference and election analysis (eiCompare), natural language processing for political text, and AI model development with the Allen Institute for AI (Ai2).

Graduate Training

The ISRR supports UNM Political Science graduate students working at the intersection of rights, repression, and quantitative/computational methods.

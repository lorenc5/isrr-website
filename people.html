People

Faculty directors, affiliated graduate students, and collaborators of the Institute for Study of Rights and Repression.

Directors

Loren Collingwood

Professor, Political Science · Co-Director, ISRR

Loren Collingwood is Professor of Political Science and Director of the Departmental Honors Program at the University of New Mexico. His research focuses on American politics with emphasis on race and ethnic politics, particularly Latino politics and immigration. He is the author of Campaigning in a Racially Diversifying America: When and How Cross-Racial Electoral Mobilization Works (Oxford University Press, 2020) and co-author of Sanctuary Cities: The Politics of Refuge (Oxford University Press, 2019).

Collingwood is the lead developer of the eiCompare R package, which provides tools for measuring racially polarized voting and has been used in voting rights litigation nationwide. His research has appeared in the British Journal of Political Science, Election Law Journal, Electoral Studies, American Politics Research, Journal of Race and Ethnic Politics, and other outlets.

Education: PhD, University of Washington (2012); BA, California State University, Chico

lcollingwood@unm.edu · Google Scholar

Sarah Dreier

Assistant Professor, Political Science · Co-Director, ISRR

Sarah K. Dreier is Assistant Professor of Political Science at the University of New Mexico. Her research examines human rights and state oppression, gender, religion, and democratic backsliding, with fieldwork in East Africa, Northern Ireland, and Western Europe. Her book project examines rights for women and sexual minorities in East Africa, focusing on how transnational churches shape human rights outcomes.

Dreier is co-PI on the $152 million NSF- and NVIDIA-funded Allen Institute for AI (Ai2) Open Multimodal AI Infrastructure to Accelerate Science project, where she co-leads data curation. Her methodological work integrates qualitative methods with natural language processing and large language model evaluation for political text. She has published in the Journal of Politics, British Journal of Political Science, International Studies Quarterly, Journal of Human Rights, Democratization, and Harvard Data Science Review.

Education: PhD, University of Washington (2019); BA, Northwestern University

Previous positions: NSF Postdoctoral Fellow (Political Science & Computer Science), eScience Data Science Fellow (University of Washington), World Bank Consultant, Center for American Progress (Washington, D.C.)

skdreier@unm.edu · Personal Website · Google Scholar

Research Areas

ISRR research is organized into two substantive pillars and a shared methodological program.

Voting Rights, Redistricting & Immigration Policy

Lead: Loren Collingwood

Voting Rights & Racially Polarized Voting

A core focus of ISRR is the empirical measurement of racially polarized voting (RPV) and its implications for minority representation under the Voting Rights Act. Collingwood and collaborators have developed and applied ecological inference methods in voting rights cases across the United States, providing expert analysis and open-source tools (see eiCompare) for courts, advocacy organizations, and researchers.

Redistricting & Representation

The Institute studies how electoral district boundaries shape political representation for racial and ethnic minorities. This includes analysis of redistricting plans, the measurement of geographic and demographic patterns in voter behavior, and the development of quantitative standards for evaluating proposed maps.

Immigration Policy, Sanctuary Cities & Immigrant Detention

Building on the book Sanctuary Cities: The Politics of Refuge (Oxford, 2019), this research stream examines the politics of local immigration enforcement, the adoption and consequences of sanctuary policies, and public opinion toward immigration across racial and ethnic groups.

A related line of research investigates the expansion of immigrant detention in the United States, including the role of private prison industries in shaping punitive immigration policy. Key publications include "Expanding Carceral Markets: Detention Facilities, ICE Contracts, and the Financial Interests of Punitive Immigration Policy" (Race and Social Problems, 2018) and "Cosponsoring and Cashing In: US House Members' Support for Punitive Immigration Policy and Financial Payoffs from the Private Prison Industry" (Business and Politics, 2021). This work examines how financial interests in detention infrastructure influence legislative behavior and the broader politics of immigration enforcement.

Race, Ethnicity & Political Behavior

Broader work under this pillar includes the study of cross-racial electoral mobilization, Latino political behavior, and the conditions under which diverse coalitions form in American elections, as examined in Campaigning in a Racially Diversifying America (Oxford, 2020).

In "Walls, Weed, and Coal: How Threats to Local Industry Shape Economic Voting" (Political Behavior, 2025), Collingwood, Morín, and Tzelgov examine how voters prioritize candidates who protect local economic interests tied to specific industries. In Texas's 28th Congressional District — a 75–80% Hispanic border district stretching from San Antonio to Laredo — the study finds that voters in areas with large shares of protective services workers and proximity to Border Patrol headquarters and ICE facilities disproportionately supported incumbent Henry Cuellar over progressive challenger Jessica Cisneros in the 2020 and 2022 Democratic primaries, reflecting economic self-interest tied to immigration enforcement infrastructure. The paper extends this framework to California's marijuana legalization vote and West Virginia's partisan shift away from Democrats as coal became a political liability.

Human Rights, State Repression & Democratic Backsliding

Lead: Sarah Dreier

East Africa: Gender, Religion & Rights

Dreier's first book project examines rights for women and sexual minorities in East Africa. This research investigates how transnational churches shape human rights outcomes — analyzing the interplay of religion, gender politics, and state policy through extensive fieldwork and archival research.

Northern Ireland: Internment & Democratic Backsliding

This research stream analyzes Britain's use of internment without trial in Northern Ireland (1971–1975) as an episode of democratic backsliding. Drawing on declassified Prime Ministers' correspondence files, Dreier uses qualitative process tracing and NLP methods to study how governments rationalize departures from human rights commitments. Key publications include "Rationalizing Backsliding: Internment in Northern Ireland" (Journal of Human Rights, 2025).

NLP for Human Rights Text

A methodological extension of this work develops and evaluates natural language processing approaches for classifying political concepts in government documents. "Troubles in Text" (Journal of Politics, 2025) demonstrates both the potential and limitations of using NLP to detect government rationalizations for rights abuses, offering practical guidance for computational text analysis in political science.

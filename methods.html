Methods & Software

ISRR faculty contribute to methodological innovation through open-source statistical software and large-scale AI research infrastructure. Our tools and methods are designed to serve both academic researchers and legal practitioners.

eiCompare Suite

R Packages

Loren Collingwood & collaborators

The eiCompare suite is a family of R packages for voting rights research, providing tools for measuring racially polarized voting (RPV), ecological inference, and supporting redistricting and Voting Rights Act litigation. The suite has been used in voting rights cases across the country.

eiCompare

The core package implements and compares ecological inference methods — including EI and RxC — providing a unified framework for legal practitioners, expert witnesses, and researchers. Developed through applied work on cases such as East Ramapo, it builds on and extends packages including ei, eiPack, wru, and censusxy.

CRAN GitHub R Journal Article

eiExpand

Augments eiCompare's RPV functionality with streamlined analyses and visualizations designed for voting rights and redistricting litigation. Implements methods described in Barreto, Collingwood, Garcia-Rios, and Oskooii (2022), "Estimating Candidate Support in Voting Rights Act Cases" (Sociological Methods & Research).

CRAN

eiFactor5

Provides tools for totality of circumstances Factor 5 analysis using Census ACS data, supporting the evidentiary framework used in Voting Rights Act litigation.

AI Research with Ai2

NLP / AI

Sarah Dreier & Allen Institute for AI

Dreier is co-PI on the Open Multimodal AI Infrastructure to Accelerate Science project, a $152 million initiative funded by the National Science Foundation and NVIDIA and led by the Allen Institute for AI (Ai2). She is the only social scientist among the five principal investigators.

Her role focuses on data curation — identifying and preparing diverse data types relevant to scientific tasks such as research analysis and code generation. This work builds on her methodological research evaluating how well large language models handle the idiosyncratic text produced by politicians and policymakers.

The broader NLP program at ISRR develops and evaluates computational text analysis methods for political science, including supervised classification of government rationalizations for rights abuses and assessment of LLM performance on political concepts.
